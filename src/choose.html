Modules 7 and 8

Which method do I use?

Most mistakes on inference questions happen before any arithmetic: choosing a proportion when the data are means, an interval when the question asks for a test, or the wrong direction for Hₐ. Answer three questions about the problem and the guide shows the method, its conditions, and where to find the critical value.

Answer three questions

The six methods at a glance

Start with the variable. If each individual gives a category (yes/no, success/failure, prefers A), you are working with a proportion p. If each individual gives a measured number (minutes, pounds, a score), you are working with a mean μ. Then ask what the question wants: a range of plausible values (confidence interval), a verdict on a claim about one specific value (hypothesis test), or how many to sample (sample size).

Direction of Hₐ. “More than”, “increased”, “higher”, “exceeds” → >. “Less than”, “decreased”, “lower”, “fewer” → <. “Different from”, “changed”, “is not”, “differs” → \ne. A claim of “at least” or “at most” is the status quo you are challenging: it becomes H_0 (with =), and Hₐ points the other way. For example, “management claims pay is at least $500” gives H_a:\mu<500.
Common mistakes. Using the proportion formulas for a measured variable (or the reverse). Writing a confidence interval when the question asks whether the data support a claim. Using p̂ in a test’s standard error (tests use p_0). Using z^* for a mean when only s is known (use t^*).

Practice choosing

Each scenario is new. Pick the method (and, for a test, the direction of Hₐ) without computing anything. The goal is to recognize the setting quickly.
